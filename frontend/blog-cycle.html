Understanding Your Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle is a rhythm your body follows, usually between 21 and 35 days. Before ovulation, an egg is prepared; around the middle of the cycle the egg is released; later, if pregnancy does not happen, the uterine lining sheds and you get your period.

A “regular” cycle simply means it is similar for you most months, even if it is a little longer or shorter than someone else’s. Stress, sleep, weight changes, and conditions like PCOS can affect timing and flow.

Writing down when your period starts, how long it lasts, and how you feel helps you see patterns over time. This makes it easier to estimate your next period, plan around busy days, and explain concerns clearly to a doctor.
